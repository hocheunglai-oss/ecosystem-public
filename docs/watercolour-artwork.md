The owner’s latest 28 October 2026 direction rejects the flat, blurred smooth-band proposal and asks for a two-tone ocean with waves pushing against each other and visible moving layers. The earlier reference supplies the sense of depth; it is not an instruction to animate a static image. The camera and foreground stay fixed while the water surface changes shape. This document describes the current candidate and acceptance requirements; visual review and release verification are pending, and it does not claim deployment.

## Current scene and still fallback

`src/flowingWaterRenderer.ts` renders an actual depth-tested triangle mesh. The current candidate uses a 384 × 256 grid with 197,630 triangle-strip vertices. Two counter-propagating wave trains change its elevation; the fixed oblique projection turns those elevations into moving crests and depth ordering. Surface normals, reflected light and smaller wavelets give the layers form. Jade/teal and pale-blue water retain distinguishable material colours while their meeting front rises and recedes with the swells. This is a stylized fictional ocean, not footage or a full fluid-dynamics simulation. No bitmap supplies the animated geometry or colour, and motion does not come from translating, resizing or distorting an image.

## Motion, themes and branding

Integer temporal harmonics give the changing mesh elevation and lighting a seamless 48-second period, including continuous motion at the boundary. The opposing swells must visibly rise, pass and meet, giving depth beyond brightness changes on a flat plane. Keep the experience calm enough for an employee workspace. Rendering remains capped at 30 draws/second, device-pixel ratio 2 and about three million pixels. The live scene is procedural graphics, not an encoded video. Label any supplied video as a recording of the scene rather than the animation source.

Day and night share the same mesh, elevation, projected layers, framing and active clock. Night applies colour changes only: jade becomes plum, icy blue becomes dusky blue, and light crests become warmer plum highlights. The mapping follows the water and its crests, not three fixed spatial bands or a gold/copper-clamped area. Use this same static mapping for the live surface and still fallback. Do not add a separate night mesh, spatial mask or image download. Hidden tabs and open workspaces freeze the active phase and stop rendering; returning resumes at that phase. Reduced motion uses the still image. Unsupported or lost graphics retains the matching fallback and usable foreground controls.

Keep the original blended E/F letter geometry and backing logo asset unchanged, including its continuous green lower bar and white letters.
